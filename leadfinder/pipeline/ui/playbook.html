Playbook

How to turn a preview into a paying client. The call script on each lead is personalised with their name, reviews and web address; this is the full version plus pricing and setup.

1 · The daily routine

  1. Publish a batch of previews (button top-right, start with ~100 Hot), wait 2–3 minutes, press Check live.
  2. Click 📞 Call queue. It lists live previews, best leads first, skipping anyone who's had 4 tries or has a call-back that isn't due yet.
  3. Call → log the outcome with one click → it jumps to the next lead. Text the link after every conversation or voicemail (copy-SMS button).
  4. When an email arrives from the preview's “Make it mine” form, find the lead (search the business name) and move it to Interested.

2 · The call script

Rules: don't introduce yourself first, lead with their problem, mention the site already exists, and finish your first breath with a tiny, easy yes (“can I text you the link?”) — not a meeting, not a sale.

Opener (whoever answers)

“Hi! Quick one — I was looking at [Business] on Google Maps: [312] reviews at [4.8] stars — that's brilliant — but there's no website. So people who find you can't book in or see your prices, and some just ring the next place that has one.

I've actually already built you a website — it's finished and free to look at. What's the best mobile to text the link to?”

That closing question replaces “who am I speaking to?”. The owner says “send it to me”. Staff say “that'd be the owner” — and now you know who you've got without asking.

If it's the owner

“Sending it now — it'll come from this number… Got it? Have a quick look while I'm on the line, it takes 20 seconds.”
…
“What do you think? Anything you'd change — colours, photos, wording?”

Let them redesign it out loud. Every change they suggest makes it more “theirs”. Write the changes in Notes. Then:

“If you like it, I'll put it on your own address — something like [business].com.au — with your photos, hours and prices, live this week. Want me to set that up?”

If it's staff

“No worries — what's the owner's first name? I'll put it on the text so they know it's not spam.” → text it → “When's the best time to catch them? I'll give them a quick ring then.”

Log “Spoke to staff”, save the owner's name (it goes into the SMS automatically), set a call-back.

Voicemail (under 15 seconds), then text straight away

“Hi, it's [your name] — I've built a free website preview for [Business]. I'll text you the link from this number. Have a look when you get a sec. Cheers!”

Objections

  • “Not interested.” — “Totally fair. Is that because you're flat out already, or you've had a bad run with web people?” Busy → “That's exactly why — the site takes the enquiries while you're working. I'll text the link, look whenever.” Firm no → “No worries, I'll take it down. Have a good one.” Log Not interested — the preview is removed on the next publish.
  • “All our work is word of mouth.” — “Makes sense, that's why you've got the reviews. The site catches the people who hear about you and then Google you before they call.”
  • “We use Facebook / Instagram.” — “Perfect, keep doing that. The website is for people searching on Google — it links to your Facebook too.”
  • “Are you from Google? Is this a scam?” — “No — I'm not Google, and I don't want any money up front. I'm a web designer; I made you a preview to look at for free. You only pay if you want it live.” (Never say you're calling “about your Google listing” — that's what scam callers say.)
  • “Where are you based?” — Be straight: “I work online with Aussie businesses — I'm in Sri Lanka, with an Australian number. You only pay once you're happy with it.” Honesty here builds trust; being caught out kills the sale.
  • “Email it to me.” — “Sure, what's the email? I'll text it too so you can see it on your phone now.”
  • “I've already got someone doing one.” — “Great — is it live yet? No? Have a look at this one anyway. If it's better, you can have it this week.”
  • “Call me later.” — “Sure — tomorrow morning or afternoon?” → set the follow-up date.

Follow-up rhythm (max 4 tries, then let it go)

Day 0 call + SMS · Day 2 call at a different time of day · Day 5 SMS (“did you get a chance to look?”) · Day 10 last call. After that, move to Not interested.

  • Collecting hosting fees: yearly, by bank transfer, in advance. The first year goes on the website invoice; send the next one a year after launch. No monthly option until a card payment provider is set up. The Terms let you pause a site that's 30+ days overdue after two reminders.

“Can you just change this one thing?”

  • Inside their package (wording, a photo, colours): yes, it's part of a round of changes. Ask for all their changes in one list, because a round is one list.
  • Bigger than their package (a new page or a booking system on Foundation, a layout change): “Absolutely, we can do that. That level of change is in our Growth package, which is $150 more than what you have. Shall I upgrade you?” An upgrade is always just the difference.
  • Bigger than any package (a 6th page on Pro, a custom feature): quote it at $60 an hour in 30-minute blocks, in writing, before you start.
  • After launch: free small edits during their edit period (Growth 14 days, Pro 30). After that, option A clients have 30 minutes a month; everyone else is $60 an hour.

Negotiating

  1. Say the middle price first, then stop talking. “Most businesses like yours go with Growth, at $799.” Silence does the work.
  2. If they hesitate, let them name a number: “What did you have in mind?”
  3. Trade, don't discount. A lower price means less: “For $649 I can finish this page as it is: you send me the photos and wording.” Never drop the price for nothing.
  4. Get something back for any discount: pay today, yearly hosting, a Google review, a testimonial, or a referral.
  5. Make it easy to say yes: one payment is simplest; the agreement also allows half now, half before launch.
  6. Set your floor before you dial (e.g. $549) and walk away below it. Cheap clients cost the most time.
  7. Referrals: “If you know another business without a website, I'll give you $100 for each one that signs up.”

Only say “launch price for my first 10 clients” if it's true. Record what you quoted and what they agreed in each lead's Details so you learn what actually closes.

5 · Texting rules (Australia's Spam Act)

  • Every SMS must say who you are (your name + DuNous) and include an opt-out — the template already ends with “Reply STOP to opt out”.
  • Honour STOP / “don't contact me” immediately: set the lead to Do not contact (also removes the preview).
  • Text after a call or voicemail rather than blasting cold texts — it's both safer legally and far more effective.
  • Landlines (no 📱) can't receive SMS.

6 · Phone setup

Zoiper dropping calls or one-way audio

  • Switch transport from UDP to TCP (or TLS if Crazytel supports it on your account): Zoiper → Accounts → your account → Advanced / Network. UDP SIP through Sri Lankan home routers and ISPs is the most common cause of one-way audio and dropped registrations.
  • Turn on STUN (e.g. stun.l.google.com:19302) and keep-alive; set registration expiry to 60–120 seconds.
  • On your router, turn SIP ALG off if it has the option.
  • Codecs: put G.711 (alaw/ulaw) first, turn video off. Use wired ethernet or strong Wi-Fi; a VPN only if your ISP blocks SIP (it adds delay).
  • The green call button in the tracker is a sip: link, so it dials through Zoiper. Zoiper normally registers itself for those links when it's installed. If clicking it doesn't open Zoiper, use “Copy number” and paste the number into Zoiper. The small tel: button uses FaceTime or your iPhone instead.
  • If Zoiper still misbehaves, try another softphone with the same Crazytel login (Linphone is free on Mac) — that tells you whether the problem is the app or the connection.

Number showing as “Spam” / “Scam likely”

  • Those labels mostly come from call-screening apps (Hiya on Samsung phones, Truecaller) and the carriers' own filters, based on lots of short or unanswered calls and people blocking you.
  • Register the number as a business with Hiya and Truecaller and ask for the label to be reviewed.
  • Call less, better: roughly 40–60 dials a day per number, let it ring 20–25 seconds, never call the same number twice in one day, and don't hang up the instant someone answers.
  • Say your business name in voicemails and texts so people save you rather than block you. A text after the voicemail makes your next call recognisable.

FaceTime / iMessage

Not possible on a Crazytel number — Apple only registers numbers on an iPhone SIM. Use WhatsApp Business on the Australian number instead (verify with the “Call me” option and answer in Zoiper), then WhatsApp Desktop on your Mac. The previews already offer WhatsApp as the “prefer to chat?” option.
